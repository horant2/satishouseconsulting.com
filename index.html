The playbook

The same five moves, in the same order, at a $5 million machine shop or a $300 million portfolio company. The difference is only how many suppliers are on the list.

  1. Map every dollar

    Export the ledger, clean it, and put every supplier in a category. Most owners are surprised twice: by how many suppliers they have and by how few of them matter.

  2. Rank the suppliers who matter

    Usually fifteen to sixty suppliers carry two thirds of the spend. Each one gets a file: contract, terms, price history, alternatives, leverage. Nothing else gets touched until these are handled.

  3. Compete or negotiate every category

    Freight, packaging, fuel, maintenance supplies, telecom, insurance, professional services, raw materials. Bid what can be bid. Negotiate what cannot. Sign contracts so the price holds.

  4. Fix the terms and free the cash

    Early-pay arrangements nobody remembers agreeing to, thirty-day terms that could be forty-five, deposits that never needed to be paid. Cash usually moves before the savings do.

  5. Put a process and an agent in place, then leave

    A procurement policy people follow, an approval flow that takes minutes, and one artificial intelligence agent doing the repetitive work: invoice matching, quote comparison, contract review. Your team runs it. I built my own platform this way and use it on every engagement.
